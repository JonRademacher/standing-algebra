# Full solve attempt

## Target

Prove the Riemann Hypothesis.

## Step 1 — Assume the negation

Assume that at least one nontrivial zero lies off the critical line.

## Step 2 — Produce a finite negative witness

By the Li/Bombieri–Lagarias detector reconstructed in Contracts 04–05, the negation of RH implies that there exists a positive integer n with

\[
\lambda_n<0.
\]

At the scholarly theorem level, this detector lane is available. Formal transcription remains unfinished.

## Step 3 — Apply the independent barrier candidate

Contract 06 evaluates the candidate trace architecture whose intended conclusion is

\[
\lambda_n\ge0
\qquad\text{for every }n\ge1.
\]

If established independently, the selected n would satisfy both

\[
\lambda_n<0
\quad\text{and}\quad
\lambda_n\ge0,
\]

which is impossible.

## Step 4 — Failure

The universal barrier has not been reconstructed or independently validated. In particular, the proof requires all three of the following:

1. **A2** — the special test-function family converges to exactly twice the Li coefficient;
2. **A3** — the first trace component vanishes;
3. **A5** — the second trace component is nonnegative, including vanishing of the cross-term trace.

A4, positivity of the underlying Gram-form operator, is not enough by itself because the one-sided projected product is not automatically positive.

## Terminal state
