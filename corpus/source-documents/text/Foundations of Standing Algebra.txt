Foundations of Standing Algebra: A Formal Companion to On Relevance

Introduction and Summary of the Formal System

Abstract

This work introduces Standing Algebra (Σᴿ), a manysorted firstorder logical framework designed to formalize standing, autonomy, recognition, and structural legitimacy within multiagent systems. Developed as the mathematical counterpart to the structural theory advanced in On Relevance, Σᴿ provides a rigorous axiomatic foundation for analyzing how agents gain, lose, preserve, or distort standing within pluralistic environments.

At the core of the system is the Successor Axiom, which establishes standing as an additive, nondilutive resource acquired through the transition from nullity to recognition. Using a successorbased standing function, Σᴿ formally distinguishes prime entities (autonomy singularities with irreducible standing) from composite entities (structural or relational aggregates) via explicit firstorder definitions. This partition yields an algebra that generalizes classical successor arithmetic, monoidal composition, and degreebased graph structure into a unified analytic space.

A second tier of supplemental axioms incorporates structural principles from the broader framework—CapacityIndexed Autonomy (CIA), the AutonomyLimiting Reflex Principle (ALRP), the NonReciprocity Prevention Principle (NRPP), Standing Test Condition 5 (STC5), rerunnability, bounded drift, and directed repair. These axioms constrain the admissibility and legitimacy of transformations on agents, defining mathematically what it means for an operation to be noncoercive, autonomypreserving, and structurally symmetric.

Taken together, these components form a complete algebraic and logical system capable of formally characterizing domination, recognition failure, coercive coupling, structural asymmetry, and autonomy preservation. The resulting framework has direct applications in the design and verification of AI alignment and safety architectures, governance models, distributed systems, organizational analysis, and any domain requiring a formally principled account of pluralistic coordination.

Standing Algebra thus provides the first generalpurpose mathematical model of nondomination and structural legitimacy, offering an autonomypreserving alternative to utilitybased or rewardbased paradigms in multiagent reasoning and AI system design.

Introduction

This volume presents the formal mathematical foundations underlying the analytic framework developed in On Relevance: The Social Physics of Pluralism. While the primary text focuses on standing, autonomy, legitimacy, pluralism, and structural coordination as conceptual and philosophical constructs, the present companion work provides a rigorous mathematical system capable of expressing those same structures in a precise, axiomatic form.

The goal of this book is not to mathematize philosophy for its own sake, nor to convert sociopolitical structures into mechanical abstractions. Rather, it is to provide a formal substrate—a logically coherent algebra and manysorted firstorder framework—through which the architecture of standing, recognition, and autonomy can be modeled with clarity, consistency, and analytic depth.

The development presented here is called Standing Algebra (Σᴿ). It is a system that generalizes successorbased arithmetic, graphtheoretic coupling structures, and algebraic monoids into a unified formal language. It captures three essential categories of agents:

Null entities (those without standing),

Prime entities (autonomy singularities with irreducible standing), and

Composite entities (structural or relational composites made of standing increments or couplings).

In addition, this system incorporates formal representations of:

Autonomy as a monotonic function of capacity,

Noncoercion as a constraint on admissible operations,

Legitimacy as standingpreservation under symmetric conditions,

Drift as bounded perturbation,

Repair as directed symmetry restoration, and

Rerunnability as a fixedpoint requirement.

These elements, which appear conceptually in On Relevance, are given explicit firstorder logical formulations in this companion text.

The result is a selfcontained mathematical framework that can be studied independently, extended by mathematicians and logicians, or used to analyze any system of pluralistic agents, standing relations, or structural couplings.

Summary of the Formal System (Standing Algebra, Σᴿ)

Standing Algebra (Σᴿ) is developed in manysorted firstorder logic with the following essential features:

1. Multiple Domains (Sorts)

Standing Algebra uses several sorts to distinguish between:

U — entities or agents

N — standing values

C — capacities

D — degrees of coupling

O — operations or transformations

This allows the system to remain firstorder while expressing rich structural relations.

2. Core Functions and Relations

The system is built on a small set of primitive operators:

A standing function

A successor function

An additive operation

A degree function

A capacity function

Optional entitycomposition operator

And relations such as:

Null(i) — entity lacks standing

Prime(i) — entity is an autonomy singularity

Composite(i) — entity has composite standing

Coupled(i,j) — relational coupling

Admissible(F) — allowed transformations

Legitimate(F) — noncoercive, standingpreserving operations

Drift(F) and Repair(F) — structural dynamics

3. Tier 1 Axioms: The Standing Algebra (Σᴿ₁)

Tier 1 defines the foundational arithmetic of standing:

Nullity (entities with zero standing)

Successor (the first unit of standing)

Additive primacy (standing grows through successor increments)

Nondilution (standing cannot decrease under addition)

Prime entities (σ=1 and degree=0)

Composite entities (σ>1 or degree>0)

Partition of all agents into null, prime, or composite

Monotonic successor growth

No return to nullity

This forms a commutative, standingbased monoid enriched with couplingdegree information.

4. Tier 2 Axioms: Structural Legitimacy Algebra (Σᴿ₂)

Tier 2 encodes the structural constraints central to On Relevance:

CIA (capacityindexed autonomy): standing must track capacity

ALRP (autonomylimiting reflex): no operation can reduce another’s standing

NRPP (nonreciprocity prevention): asymmetrical dependence is illegitimate

STC5 (standing test condition): only standingpreserving maps are legitimate

Rerunnability: operations must be idempotent under symmetric conditions

Directed repair: repair must increase standing and reduce coercive coupling

Drift: perturbations are bounded and nondestructive

Legitimacy: an operation is legitimate iff it is successorconsistent and noncoercive

These axioms elevate Σᴿ from a standing arithmetic to a structural theory of autonomy and legitimacy, suitable for analyzing political, social, computational, and epistemic systems.
