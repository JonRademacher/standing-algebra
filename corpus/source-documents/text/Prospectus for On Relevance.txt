Prospectus for On Relevance: The Social Physics of Pluralism

Brief Description

On Relevance develops a unified structural framework for evaluating standing, legitimacy, coordination, and autonomy across all domains of pluralist society. It is not a normative doctrine but a metaparadigmatic analytic engine that identifies the structural constraints any claim, action, institution, or system must satisfy in order to bind diverse agents without domination. The framework is built from foundational axioms of interaction, derivative standing, and symmetry, from which it derives a series of structural laws analogous to principles of thermodynamics or systems theory. These tools allow readers to diagnose when authority becomes domination, when coordination fails, and when repair or redesign is required.

The book’s approach is rigorously crossdomain. It applies the same portable analytic mechanics to perception, morality, language, politics, economics, ecology, technology, identity, culture, science, conflict, and global concerns. Its pedagogy emphasizes rerunnability, symmetry, reparability, and standingevaluation, giving readers a method they can use across disciplines. The result is a coherent structural grammar for pluralist coordination that is intellectually ambitious while remaining clear, accessible, and practically applicable for multiple fields.

Outstanding Features

A unified analytic engine (Coupling → Relevance → Autonomy Impact → Standing Evaluation → Legitimate Constraint → Repair) usable across all domains without metaphysical assumptions.

Foundational axioms and structural laws giving philosophy a rigor analogous to frameworks in systems theory, engineering, and physics.

A crossdomain architecture spanning perception, morality, politics, ecology, economics, technology, identity, science, conflict, and global history.

A valueneutral, nonmoralized method grounded in structural constraints rather than doctrine, ideology, or tradition.

A hierarchy of autonomyimpact that resolves conflicts between claims by structural necessity instead of subjective values.

Reconstruction-focused design: emphasizes repair, restoration, corrigibility, and longterm system stability.

Full glossary, appendices, and quick-reference sections making the framework teachable and operational.

Original contributions to standing theory, legitimacy, non-domination, autonomy, and pluralist coordination.

Competition

Closest domain relatives (but not true competitors):

Philip Pettit — Republicanism / Just Freedom
Strength: strong theory of non-domination.
Weakness: domain-bound (political), lacks cross-domain portability; relies on moral-political commitments.

Gerald Gaus — The Order of Public Reason
Strength: pluralist structure for public justification.
Weakness: tied to moral justificatory liberalism; does not generalize to perception, technology, science, identity, or ecology.

John Rawls — A Theory of Justice / Political Liberalism
Strength: foundational for political liberal theory.
Weakness: normative platform-dependent; cannot function as a structural engine.

Amartya Sen — The Idea of Justice
Strength: capability-based analysis of injustice.
Weakness: not a structural theory of legitimacy; lacks cross-domain analytic formalism.

Luciano Floridi — The Philosophy of Information
Strength: information-centric ontology and ethics.
Weakness: metaphysical commitments; does not provide standing/legitimacy mechanics.

Daniel Dennett — Intentional systems, cognitive scaffolding
Strength: structural-functional insights.
Weakness: not a social-coordination framework or legitimacy engine.

Elinor Ostrom — Governing the Commons
Strength: empirical grounding, multi-level governance.
Weakness: domain-limited (commons governance), lacks axiomatic generality.

How On Relevance differs

It is not a political theory, moral theory, or institutional theory.

It is a structural meta-framework capable of analyzing all domains symmetrically.

It provides a single rerunnable engine for legitimacy across systems of any type.

It avoids metaphysical, ethical, or ideological commitments.

It unifies perception, agency, autonomy, language, and social systems under one analytic grammar.

Why another book is needed

No existing work provides:

an axiomatic structural basis for legitimacy

cross-domain standing analysis

a repair-based model of nondomination

a unified formalism connecting perception, coordination, and institutional design

Thus, this book fills a structural gap across multiple disciplines simultaneously.

Authors

Jonathan Rademacher is an independent philosopher developing structural frameworks for legitimacy, autonomy, and pluralist coordination. His work draws on systems analysis, cognitive science, political theory, and epistemology to generate cross-domain structural tools for evaluating standing and non-domination. He has produced extensive foundational axioms, structural laws, and analytic formalism in On Relevance, integrating insights across perception, morality, technology, identity, conflict, and global history.

He is particularly suited to write this work due to its reliance on conceptual synthesis, cross-disciplinary integration, and structural rigor — areas aligned with his longstanding personal research trajectory.
